# ADR-019: Managed Registry Snapshot Materialization and Resource-Control Boundaries

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-09-25
- **Decision owners:** MetricShell maintainers
- **Related research:** `INV-019 — Performance, Snapshot Materialization and Resource Limits`
- **Evidence:** [INV-019 report](../../../research/INV-019/report.md)
- **Core dependency:** [ADR-004](ADR-004.md)
- **Semantic dependency:** [ADR-016](ADR-016.md)
- **Concurrency dependency:** [ADR-017](ADR-017.md)
- **Transport dependency:** [ADR-018](ADR-018.md)

## Context

Managed Aggregation accepts individual instrumentation operations but must still enter MetricShell Core through the
existing complete-candidate-snapshot boundary. The registry therefore needs a materialization strategy that preserves
one registry-wide committed generation during concurrent mutation and scraping, avoids rebuilding unchanged output,
and has bounded resource exposure.

INV-019 compared encoding after every mutation, materialization on scrape, a generation-based immutable encoded cache,
and copy-on-write registry state. Matching-fingerprint reference ARM64 and Ubuntu x86_64 runs each passed 20/20 portable
assertions and E-019.1–E-019.7. Full-body linked-family checks observed zero mixed-generation responses. Both runs also
confirmed exact bounded queue admission, visible rejection, pre-mutation series/bucket limit rejection, fatal cgroup
OOM separation and successful execution with the retained FD limit. Throughput, latency, RSS and encoding timings
differed by environment and are observations only.

## Decision

### Generation-based immutable encoded snapshot cache

Managed Registry uses a generation-based immutable encoded snapshot cache.

Registry mutation and snapshot materialization are separate stages:

1. ADR-017's bounded single owner serializes and commits accepted registry mutations.
2. A materialization reads one complete registry-wide committed generation.
3. The resulting encoded representation is immutable and is associated with that generation.
4. An unchanged generation may reuse its already materialized immutable representation.
5. A newly committed mutation makes the cached generation stale. The next required materialization builds from a new
   complete eligible committed generation.

Several committed mutations may coalesce before materialization. MetricShell does not guarantee publication of every
intermediate registry generation.

### Concurrent response ownership

Every concurrent or slow scraper receives immutable response bytes belonging to one complete generation. Newer commits
or later cache replacement do not modify bytes already handed to a reader. A correct generation header alone is
insufficient: the complete encoded body must represent the same registry-wide generation.

### Bounded resource controls

Managed Registry has independent bounded controls for at least:

- active series cardinality;
- finite histogram bucket count;
- owner admission queue capacity.

Exceeding a policy limit deterministically rejects the operation before changing the corresponding valid committed
state. Queue overload is an observable rejection under ADR-017; silent drop is forbidden. A fatal OS or cgroup OOM is a
process/container failure and is not reported or masked as an ordinary managed-operation rejection.

### Core boundary

Managed state enters Core only as a complete candidate snapshot. Core whole-candidate validation and atomic active-state
replacement remain governed by ADR-004 and are unchanged by this decision.

## Running Publication Policy

Managed Registry uses fixed periodic, coalesced publication. One publisher goroutine performs at most one synchronous
materialization and Core installation at a time; there is no publication queue. A tick publishes the newest eligible
complete generation, and multiple commits between ticks may coalesce. Intermediate generations are therefore not
guaranteed to become Core-visible.

This policy is selected because it bounds publication trigger rate independently of mutation rate and scrape
concurrency. Per-operation or unbounded on-mutation publication would couple every commit to full-registry
materialization and could create work proportional to mutation rate. On-scrape publication would repeat expensive work
on concurrent scrapes and put materialization latency directly on the scrape path. A queued publication per mutation
would add an independently growing resource. The fixed publisher instead has bounded concurrency, no backlog and
preserves immutable complete-generation installation.

The initial product default is `1s`. It limits normal operation to at most one publication attempt per second, allows
short mutation bursts to coalesce and makes long-running workload updates visible without waiting for completion. This
is an operational starting point, not a promise that every generation is visible within one second.

The supported range is `10ms` through `1m`:

- `10ms` is the lower safety boundary because it caps triggers at 100 per second while the single synchronous
  publisher still prevents concurrent materializations and queued publication work. Correctness at this boundary is
  covered by the managed end-to-end publication test. INV-019 does not establish portable throughput or CPU headroom
  at `10ms`; the bound is a concurrency/resource-shape guarantee, not a capacity claim.
- `1m` is the upper product boundary because it permits substantial coalescing for large or high-churn registries while
  retaining periodic live publication rather than degrading silently into final-only publication.

Registry size, encoded snapshot byte limits and materialization cost determine the work of each attempt. Prometheus
scrape cadence determines how often a new installed generation can be observed, but scrapes do not trigger managed
materialization. Operators should choose an interval no shorter than their resource budget and useful observation
cadence; operators with large or high-churn registries should increase it. The default and range are product settings,
not SLAs, SLOs, benchmark claims or universal capacity limits.

## Other Numeric Limit Boundaries

The research tested through 20,000 active series, 100 histogram buckets and owner queue capacity 1,024. These values are
coverage endpoints, not production defaults or universal maxima. Measured operations per second, p50/p95/p99 latency,
RSS, allocation and encoding timings are environment-sensitive observations, not SLAs or portable guarantees.

Exact production defaults and maxima other than the running-publication interval remain deferred until an explicit
selection rule accounts for:

- memory budget;
- maximum encoded-response budget;
- materialization latency objective;
- queue-residence and overload objective;
- label, name and family shape;
- scrape behavior;
- documented safety margin.

## Alternatives

### Encode after every mutation

Rejected as the selected design. Its work scales with both mutation rate and full registry encoding cost even when no
scraper needs a new representation.

### Materialize on every scrape

Retained as a viable fallback but not selected. It avoids mutation-time encoding but repeats full work for unchanged
generations and concurrent scrapes.

### Publish on every mutation or through an unbounded queue

Rejected. Both couple publication work to mutation rate; the queue form also introduces unbounded backlog and stale
work. Fixed periodic publication bounds concurrency and naturally coalesces superseded generations.

### Copy-on-write registry state per mutation

Rejected as tested for the bounded single-owner model because cloning cost scales with registry size on every mutation.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Complete encoded responses have one registry-wide generation.
- Unchanged generations reuse immutable materialized bytes.
- Slow readers cannot observe later mutation through already issued bytes.
- Mutation admission, commit and materialization boundaries remain explicit.
- Series, histogram and queued-work risks are independently bounded and observable.
- Policy rejection preserves committed state and remains distinguishable from fatal OOM.

### Trade-offs

- Materialization may skip intermediate committed generations.
- Registry state and one or more reader-owned immutable encodings may coexist in memory.
- Staleness tracking and immutable-response lifetime require explicit implementation discipline.
- Publication cadence must be tuned when registry size, churn, snapshot size or materialization cost increases.

## Decision Boundaries

This ADR does not change counter, gauge, histogram, descriptor or epoch semantics from ADR-016; serialization, commit
order, successful-ACK meaning, idempotency or backpressure semantics from ADR-017; or local transport from ADR-018. It
does not change Core semantics from ADR-004.

Lifecycle ownership, freeze, in-flight handling, final snapshot publication and shutdown behavior are governed by
ADR-020. The running-publication interval decision does not turn environment-sensitive INV-019 measurements into
portable guarantees. Benchmark follow-up suggestions are not requirements of this ADR.
